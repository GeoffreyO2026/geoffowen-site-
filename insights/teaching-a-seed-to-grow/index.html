Modern Parenting Is Like Trying to Teach a Seed to Grow

← All Insights

Modern parenting is like trying to teach a seed to grow.

You don’t build a seed into a plant. You stop getting in its way.

I see it constantly from parenting coaches and leadership coaches alike. Build your child’s confidence. Instil courage in them. Model resilience so it rubs off.

I think every one of these phrases carries a hidden assumption almost nobody ever actually examines, not because a parent has consciously decided it, but because of something closer to blind conditioning, a belief absorbed so early and so completely that it never gets questioned at all. Nobody sits down and decides their child’s courage is missing. It’s simply assumed, quietly and unwittingly, the same automatic way we assume a child needs to be taught table manners or taught to read, without ever pausing to ask whether this particular thing, courage, actually works the same way.

The moment a parent starts trying to build it, teach it, or model it into existence, without ever having consciously chosen to believe it wasn’t there in the first place, they end up confirming that same unexamined assumption anyway, to themselves and, more importantly, to their child.

A seed doesn’t need anyone to teach it how to grow. Everything required is already coded into it before it ever breaks the surface. What it needs is soil, water, light, the right conditions. Nothing more. Certainly nobody standing over it, coaching it toward growth.

A child is not fundamentally different. Courage, resilience, genuine confidence, these aren’t skills missing at birth and installed later through the right parental technique. They’re already present, the same way the capacity to walk or to speak is already present, waiting for the right environment, not the right instruction.

The moment a parent starts trying to instil courage, something quietly shifts. Less trust. More management. Less real responsibility handed over. More manufactured challenge designed to “build” what was supposedly missing. And a child who is constantly being built up rarely gets the one thing that would have revealed the courage already there: genuine trust, real responsibility, and enough space to actually encounter difficulty without being rescued from it.

There’s an older word we don’t reach for as often now. Reared. Worth sitting with, because reared, in its truest sense, has nothing to do with building or installing anything. An animal rears its young by providing exactly what that offspring’s own growth already depends on, food, warmth, protection, the right environment, nothing more. A mother animal isn’t creating courage or resilience in her young. She’s simply supporting what was already coded into them to unfold on its own.

Raise is a different kind of word entirely. We raise a barn. We raise the temperature. We raise the bar. Every one of those is a creative act, building something up that wasn’t there before, or wasn’t at that level yet. I think that’s precisely the assumption modern parenting has quietly absorbed, that a child needs raising in this sense, actively built up, courage and confidence constructed rather than simply already present, waiting for the environment every other young creature’s growth already depends on.

And here’s what tends to happen to a child raised this way, built up rather than genuinely reared. Confidence, resilience, courage, whatever value a parent decides needs constructing, the pattern tends to repeat. The child rarely ends up feeling the thing that was supposedly built into them. They end up performing it. Standing tall, speaking up, taking the risk, all the visible behaviour intact. Underneath it, often, sits the same quiet uncertainty the whole intervention was trying to solve, because the quality was never actually theirs to feel. It was built to satisfy someone else’s belief that it was missing.

You don’t build a seed into a plant. You stop getting in its way.
